# Renovation feasibility: apply only what changes the proposed design

Use this reference to identify the few checks that could invalidate a concept or materially change cost. The rows identify questions, not jurisdiction-independent rules or instructions to perform regulated work. Verify current local requirements and product manuals before asserting a requirement.

## Early intake: confirm only what changes the next step

Collect facts from supplied files, links and context first. Do not ask the user to repeat visible information, fill out a comprehensive questionnaire or approve an inferred plan before ordinary research. Ask for the smallest missing set, with the recommended next input first and free-form correction allowed.

| Topic | Ask early when | Sufficient starting point / useful question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intended result | The request could mean structural assessment, service mapping, a layout concept, purchase feasibility or a construction/cost plan | “I suggest starting with a feasibility concept and evidence gaps. Is your main goal layout options, wall/service assessment, or preparing a real renovation?” Do not ask if the goal is already clear. |
| Correct property and unit | Records cover several wings/floors/apartments, or there is no identifiable target | Property identifier/address or an anonymized correct plan, plus floor/wing/unit location. Ask which apartment before lengthy comparison; show a labelled locator crop if that makes the answer easier. |
| Usable baseline | No drawing, listing link or measured sketch is available | “An existing plan is the best start; a listing link or measured sketch is also useful. What do you have?” A listing is not mandatory, and lack of an exact address need not prevent generic/anonymized concept work. |
| Dimension reliability | The task depends on appliance fit, room area or exact clearances and scale is absent/contradictory | Ask for one or two known wall spans or measured dimensions, and total area/definition if known. Request ceiling heights or levels only if they affect this design. Area alone is not an independent length measurement. |
| Change boundaries and priorities | An iteration could move a fixed wall/service or trade away an important use | Establish what must stay, the few must-have functions, and which preferences may flex. Ask about budget, timing or occupancy when those change the selected scope/sequence; they are not prerequisites for a narrow drawing review. |
| Current versus historic state | Plans conflict with photos or recent alterations may invalidate the evidence | Ask which changes are known and which record reflects the current apartment. Keep both sources visible rather than quietly choosing the convenient geometry. |
| Jurisdiction and responsibility | Giving location-dependent legal/approval advice or planning shared-service work | Country/municipality and tenure/owner-association context, if not already available. Do not demand these for a purely geometric exercise. |
| Research route | A focused lookup found no usable records, or further work would become an open-ended property/archival search | Explain what was found and the specific gap. Ask whether the user can supply the missing source or prefers further archive research versus a more limited concept. Do not invent access credentials or contact third parties without authorization. |

### Research stop / pivot rule

After an initial focused discovery pass, do not repeatedly search variants of an uncertain address, floor, construction date or suspected drawing title without new evidence. Present what is known, what is missing, why it affects the requested outcome and the most useful next input. This is a decision point, not a universal timer or an approval ritual. A task explicitly asking for archival investigation can justify further searching within its authorized scope.

If a preference question is optional, continue reasonable independent work and state assumptions when needed. If a missing fact is essential, leave that part unresolved; silence is not confirmation. Reuse answers across turns and ask again only when new contradictory evidence materially changes the question.

### Evidence should limit the claim, not all progress

| Inputs available | Useful work now | What remains conditional |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No plan or identified property | Brief, source request and explicitly generic concept/advice | Registered apartment plan, measured fit, wall/route classification |
| Listing/photo plan only | Room/opening identification, approximate annotations and priorities | Hidden structure/services and precise scale unless independently supported |
| Architectural or services drawing | Registered baseline, wall IDs, documented fixtures/routes, evidence gaps and supported provisional assessments | Unsupported structural classifications or concealed paths |
| Applicable structural/services records | Source-linked wall conclusions and physically documented utility layers | Current as-built agreement, unknown routes and construction authorization |

Do not use an incomplete set as a blanket reason to omit known walls, service points or a well-supported documented partition finding. Ask for targeted missing evidence rather than making the user gather every possible record before receiving useful work.

## Feasibility topics

| Topic / trigger | Establish early | Consequence for the concept |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal wall opening/removal | Current structural drawings, joist bearings/joints, beams, roof/attic posts, stability, wall material and later alterations. An architect's/services drawing rarely resolves all of these. | Reserve room for posts, beam depth and ceiling changes if unresolved. Do not classify a thin wall as safely removable. |
| Moving a kitchen, sink or laundry machine | Actual soil/waste stack, connection level, fall over the route, pipe diameter, floor build-up, access, isolation and leak containment. Confirm shared ownership. | A short horizontal distance is not proof that gravity drainage fits. A raised floor, different fixture position or intrusive connection may be necessary; do not quietly assume a pump. |
| Wet room alteration | Existing waterproofing, floor drain and falls, penetrations, ventilation, threshold, records of recent work and insurer/installer requirements. | Determine whether an apparently small change compromises an existing complete system or requires broader rebuilding. |
| Hob/oven or cooking island | Electrical supply/capacity, cable route, connection requirements, extraction/recirculation distinction, duct route, noise, service voids and appliance installation/heat clearances. | Removing a hob from the wall run frees width, but the island is not automatically practical. A recirculating hood does not establish that general ventilation is adequate. |
| Fridge, dishwasher and laundry | Exact niche, hinge opening, drawer removal, appliance doors, vibration, adjacent panels, hoses, shut-offs and service/removal route. | Check access as well as footprint. Noise/vibration beside an office or bedroom can dominate the layout choice. |
| New enclosed office/bedroom | Window/radiator position, daylight, supply/extract air paths, heating, door position, acoustics/privacy and intended occupancy/use. | A glass wall shares light, but does not establish suitable ventilation, acoustic separation or that the room qualifies legally as a bedroom. |
| Windows, balcony, exterior or entrance | Current photographs and elevations; opening type/swing, thresholds, radiators, facade restrictions and any heritage constraints. | Do not replace a balcony door with an invented window or assume permission for exterior changes. |
| Fire, acoustic or shared boundaries | Escape route/door clearances, dwelling/corridor/shaft boundaries, fire stopping and acoustic function of walls, floors and penetrations. | A non-load-bearing wall may still have important functions. Check the specific boundary rather than treating all partitions alike. |
| Older building or disruptive survey | Relevant prior surveys, material hazards, damp, concealed deterioration, floor levels and prior structural/service alterations. | Target the survey to planned disturbance. Do not instruct exploratory drilling/cutting where concealed structure or services are unresolved. |
| Permissions and responsibilities | Country/municipality, ownership/tenure, association/landlord scope, shared services, heritage status, current process and any required start authorization. | Separate concept permission from authorization to build. A contractor quote or association consent is not a substitute for other applicable approvals. Verify legal statements with current primary sources. |
| Access and building operation | Delivery/lift/stair dimensions, appliance replacement, waste removal, protection, noisy hours, neighbour/association coordination and planned utility shutdowns. | Test how the work and future maintenance can actually happen, not just the finished room. Do not modify running/shared services without appropriate authorization. |
| Cost, sequence and reversibility | Which uncertain items could change the design; demolition, structure, services, floor/ceiling making-good, approvals/design fees, temporary living arrangements and procurement dependencies. | Compare cost drivers and plausible contingency categories, not fabricated precise budgets. Survey high-impact unknowns before committing to cabinetry or final finishes. Preserve repair access and reuse where practical. |
| Handover | As-built drawings, inspections/commissioning, product manuals, warranties and waterproofing/electrical/plumbing records as applicable. | Define the evidence needed to close the project; a visual concept is not construction completion. |

## Structure-specific communication

Give a wall-by-wall evidence table when structure is the user's question: observed drawing feature, inference, confidence, missing evidence, next discriminating check. Avoid aggregating all thin walls into one verdict.

Do not equate a wall parallel to apparent joists with no supporting/stabilising role. A beam, joist joint, roof post or later change may alter the load path. Upper floors can still support attic and roof loads. Originally non-bearing elements can acquire load through deformation; describe this as a possibility to investigate, not a diagnosis.

Suggested escalation is targeted: for example, “confirm whether attic-floor joists end or join on W2 and whether a roof post bears above it,” rather than a repeated generic warning.

## Research efficiency

Start with the supplied archive set, owner/association records and current listing/photo evidence. Search external sources for questions those cannot answer: construction era/context, available structural records, current local process and manufacturer installation envelopes. Cache each fact with its URL/file, date, exact applicability and confidence. Prefer primary sources; do not turn listing text or a historical rule into proof of current compliance.

Only research products deeply when selecting products is part of the request. Verifying a 60 cm cabinet format does not require a general shopping comparison, budget questionnaire or review audit.
